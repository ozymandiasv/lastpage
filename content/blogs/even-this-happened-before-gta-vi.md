GTA VI is finally here. Well, well, well, the memes can finally rest. We can stop saying "Even this happened before GTA VI" and perhaps move on to "Even this happened before RDR3" or GTA VII. Anyway, I am certainly not buying a game this expensive, let alone purchasing a PlayStation just to play it. I'll probably wait for a sale when it eventually arrives on PC. There have been reports suggesting a PC release in the first quarter of 2027, although Rockstar hasn't officially confirmed that. Never mind, the game looks brilliant. Like most enthusiasts who don't have access to the game yet, I've spent quite some time watching gameplay footage, comparisons and videos, and I think we have finally reached a point where water physics in games are almost, almost perfect. I always thought RDR2 had the best water physics, and for years it was my benchmark. But watching the side-by-side comparisons, I think GTA VI has finally dethroned it. A base RDR2 can finally be challenged by a base GTA VI, and that's quite an achievement considering how difficult RDR2 is to compete with even in 2026.
## Rockstar Has Raised the Bar Again
The details are what fascinate me. The number of NPCs on the beach, the way they occupy the environment, the water movement and, good lord, those vehicle accidents! I've watched some of the crash sequences repeatedly. The deformation, the physics, the way vehicles react to collisions and the overall sense of weight make them look remarkably convincing. It almost feels like watching an actual accident. Rockstar seems to have spent an extraordinary amount of time making the world feel alive rather than simply creating a large map and filling it with things to do. The environmental details, pedestrian behaviour, animations and interactions are what make these games special. And then there is the house renovation feature. Surprisingly, GTA VI seems to have introduced something that feels almost like The Sims, allowing players to renovate and customise properties. I don't know how deeply this mechanic has been implemented, but I find it interesting. I particularly loved the chapter in RDR2 where John Marston builds his house. Watching him finally construct a home after everything he had been through was one of my favourite parts of the game. Perhaps I'll enjoy doing something similar in GTA VI.
## RDR2 Still Has a Different Kind of Magic
But there is one thing I am not entirely convinced GTA VI can surpass, and that is the storytelling. RDR2 is an extraordinarily difficult game to compete with because its characters, narrative, atmosphere and emotional depth are on another level. GTA has always had entertaining stories, memorable characters and brilliant satire, but Red Dead Redemption is something different. It takes its time, lets you inhabit the world and makes you care about people who are, quite frankly, deeply flawed. Some games aren't necessarily meant to tell the same kind of story. They can be brilliant because of their world, gameplay, freedom and sheer entertainment value. Visually, however, I think the comparison is going to get interesting. A vanilla RDR2 versus vanilla GTA VI is already an impressive contest. But a heavily enhanced RDR2 running at 4K with aggressive upscaling, ray-tracing mods and high-end GPU hardware can still look like a god. I would love to see how GTA VI holds up against that kind of setup once its PC version arrives, perhaps on an RTX 5090 with the highest graphical settings. That will be a much more interesting comparison than simply putting two console screenshots side by side.
## The Engine Behind the Magic
I suspect one of Rockstar's biggest advantages is its proprietary RAGE engine, the Rockstar Advanced Game Engine. It has been developed and refined across multiple generations of games, allowing Rockstar to build systems around its particular requirements: physics, animation, vehicle handling, environmental simulation and enormous interactive worlds. This is one reason I find Rockstar's approach interesting when so many studios have moved towards Unreal Engine. Unreal Engine is an extraordinary piece of technology, and there is absolutely nothing wrong with using it. In fact, CD Projekt Red has moved its future Witcher development to Unreal Engine 5. Ubisoft has its own Anvil engine, which powers much of the Assassin's Creed series. But there is something impressive about Rockstar continuing to develop and refine its own technology rather than relying on a general-purpose engine. I wouldn't necessarily claim RAGE is objectively better or more optimised than Unreal or Anvil, because that depends on the particular game and implementation. But Rockstar clearly knows how to extract an extraordinary amount from its engine. And GTA VI looks like another demonstration of what happens when a studio has spent years developing technology specifically for the kind of worlds it wants to create.
## Now, Give Us RDR3
Anyway, I probably won't be playing GTA VI until late next year or perhaps even the year after. I'll wait for the PC version, a reasonable price and, eventually, a sale. For now, watching gameplay videos and arguing about water physics is perfectly entertaining. But while everyone is celebrating GTA VI, I have a few other requests. Rockstar, could you at least acknowledge that RDR3 is somewhere in your plans? It doesn't even have to be called Red Dead Redemption 3. Give us another story from that universe. I want to return to that world, those landscapes, those characters and that extraordinary atmosphere. And while we're at it, can somebody at 2K please acknowledge that another Mafia game is in development? I know Mafia: The Old Country has only recently arrived, but I'm already looking forward to whatever comes next.Anyway thirteen years of waiting, countless memes, multiple delays and an almost unreasonable amount of anticipation. The game finally looks like it was worth the wait.
