I have been watching football for a long time now, and I actually started watching it as a Manchester United supporter. That is a story of its own. But somewhere along the way, things changed, and there is something about Liverpool beating United that feels particularly satisfying. I don't hate United the way I hate Arsenal or Manchester City. Those two teams are a different matter altogether. But United losing to Liverpool? That feels personal, and I don't think I need to explain why. Sunday's 4-0 at Anfield was an absolute treat. What a brilliant game.
## The Game Was Won in More Ways Than the Scoreline
The opening thirteen minutes were extraordinary. United were already struggling to contain Liverpool's movement when Leny Yoro brought down Alexander Isak as the last defender and received a straight red card in the eighth minute. There is an argument about whether Isak had complete control of the ball, but the decision stood, and Liverpool made the most of it. Szoboszlai placed the resulting free-kick through the gap in United's defensive wall, and just three minutes later Florian Wirtz produced that wonderful acrobatic finish from Rio Ngumoha's delivery. Two goals, a man advantage and barely any time for United to understand what had happened. But what impressed me was that Liverpool didn't simply sit back and enjoy the advantage. They kept stretching the pitch, moving the ball quickly and finding spaces between United's defensive lines. Wirtz was particularly brilliant, constantly finding pockets of space and making things happen.
## A Performance Worth Watching Again
The second half was just as enjoyable. Bradley Barcola's pace and direct running caused United all sorts of problems, and his low cross gave Ngumoha the opportunity to make it 3-0. Then came the fourth, with Liverpool winning the ball high up the pitch, Wirtz finding the run of substitute Víctor Muñoz, and the finish putting the result beyond any possible discussion. There were technical details everywhere: the speed of Liverpool's transitions, the width created by their wingers, the movement between Wirtz and the forward line, and the way they exploited United's disorganised defensive shape. The early red card obviously changed the game, but Liverpool still had to make that advantage count, and they did so emphatically. Four goals, a clean sheet and a thoroughly entertaining Sunday afternoon. I couldn't have asked for much more. Especially against United.
