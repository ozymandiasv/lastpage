Well, well, well. Just when you think things cannot get any more embarrassing, another story emerges. According to a report by The Wire, a US lobbying firm called Mercury Public Affairs, which was hired by the Indian government at a monthly retainer of $75,000 (approximately ₹70 lakh), listed Donald Trump's congratulatory social media post about Narendra Modi becoming India's longest-serving head of government among the activities it had undertaken. Let that sink in for a moment. A post that was celebrated across India's political ecosystem as a diplomatic achievement, with people presenting Trump's words as recognition of Modi's global standing, now appears in the activity report of a firm being paid by the Indian Embassy. To be clear, the documents do not establish that Trump personally received money to publish the post, or that the message itself was commissioned by the Indian government. The firm says it disseminated the post. But even that raises an uncomfortable question: why does a country of India's size and diplomatic standing need an expensive lobbying arrangement to amplify a congratulatory message from the American President? Was this really the diplomatic achievement we were told it was, or was the celebration itself part of a carefully managed public relations exercise?
## The Money Is Only Part of the Problem
And Mercury wasn't the only firm involved. The Indian Embassy also hired SHW Partners, headed by Trump associate Jason Miller, at a monthly retainer of $150,000. Its disclosures describe arranging eight calls between Indian representatives and senior American officials during May and June 2026, discussing India-US relations. Together, the two arrangements represented $225,000 in monthly retainers. Now, hiring lobbying firms is not inherently unusual. Governments across the world employ lobbyists to build relationships, communicate policy positions and protect their diplomatic and economic interests. The Indian government has pointed out that its embassy in Washington has engaged lobbying firms since 1948. Fair enough. But that doesn't make every expenditure automatically justified. We are talking about public money, and taxpayers have every right to ask what these firms achieved, what measurable benefits India received and whether these arrangements were worth the money. The Ministry of External Affairs subsequently called the interpretation of Trump's post being paid for misleading, explaining that Mercury's filing described its role as dissemination. That clarification matters, but it doesn't eliminate the larger questions surrounding the contracts.
## Who Is Accountable to Whom?
What bothers me most is the apparent absence of accountability. Why does the government so often seem incapable of providing a straightforward explanation when uncomfortable questions emerge? Why must every criticism become an attack on the nation, every demand for transparency become political opposition, and every government expenditure be defended before anyone has even asked for an explanation? I don't care whether the government is BJP, Congress or anyone else. If public money is being spent, the public deserves answers. And the media should be asking these questions relentlessly, not treating them as an inconvenient distraction. India doesn't need Donald Trump's congratulations to establish its importance, nor should its diplomatic standing depend on how warmly an American president speaks about its Prime Minister. If the government genuinely believes these lobbying arrangements served India's interests, explain how. Show us the outcomes. Tell us what we received in return. What I find humiliating isn't that India hired American lobbyists; it is the possibility that we are spending enormous amounts of public money managing perceptions while being given very little clarity about the actual results. A government that demands unquestioning faith from its citizens should at least be willing to answer their questions.
