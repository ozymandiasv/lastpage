I sometimes find myself sitting and wondering why people are so certain about God. Not necessarily whether God exists, but how confidently we seem to know what God wants, why God created us and what we are supposed to do here. I find the whole idea increasingly difficult to comprehend when I think about the universe. Our galaxy alone contains hundreds of billions of stars, and there are hundreds of billions of galaxies in the observable universe. There are planets we will never see, stars that have already died, galaxies moving away from us and entire regions of the universe whose light will never reach Earth. And somewhere on one insignificant planet, around one ordinary star, a species evolved and began arguing about which God created it. If I were God, I would probably have made a Sun and an Earth and called it a day. Why create all of this? What is the purpose? Why create human beings in the first place? To do what exactly? To fight an army of Satan? To spend eternity singing praises? And if the purpose of creation is simply to receive admiration, isn't that a rather narcissistic arrangement? Imagine creating something whose only purpose is to tell you how wonderful you are. That's essentially an automated script running endlessly and printing, "You are the best." I find the idea difficult to reconcile with the image of an all-powerful, all-knowing creator.
## The Politics of God
But my bigger concern isn't necessarily the existence of God. It is what human beings have done with the idea of God. Religion has become one of the most effective instruments for organising people, defining identities and, unfortunately, dividing them. We have people fighting over whose God is real, whose scripture is superior, whose rituals are correct and whose interpretation deserves authority. Entire political movements have been built around religious identity. Politicians invoke God when they need votes, religious leaders invoke morality when they need followers, and ordinary people are encouraged to see one another as enemies because they belong to different communities. I sometimes wonder how many religious leaders and politicians around the world depend on this separation for their influence. We don't have a reliable global count of religious leaders, but the larger point is difficult to miss: there is an enormous institutional ecosystem built around religious authority. And when political power and religious authority begin reinforcing one another, questioning either becomes increasingly difficult. The most convenient thing about dividing people through religion is that they can be made to fight over identities while overlooking the economic and political structures that affect their everyday lives.
## Who Benefits From Keeping Us Divided?
There is a quotation commonly attributed to Napoleon Bonaparte: "Religion is what keeps the poor from murdering the rich." Whether or not Napoleon said those exact words, the sentiment captures an uncomfortable political argument. Religion can offer comfort, community, hope and a sense of purpose to people facing extraordinary difficulties. There is nothing inherently wrong with that. But it can also be used to encourage people to accept suffering as destiny, inequality as divine arrangement and obedience as virtue. Imagine people struggling with unemployment, poverty, poor education and inadequate healthcare, while political conversations are deliberately redirected towards religious divisions. Imagine workers who have more in common with one another economically than they do with the wealthy people who happen to share their religion, yet are encouraged to see those workers from another community as their greatest enemies. It is a remarkably convenient arrangement for anyone who benefits from the existing distribution of power. Religion isn't the only instrument used to divide people, nor are religious people automatically manipulated or politically ignorant. But the exploitation of religious identity is something we should be able to criticise without hesitation.
## Perhaps God Just Started the Simulation
And here is where my own position becomes a little different. I don't entirely deny the possibility that there is a God. I don't know how the universe began, why there is something rather than nothing, or whether there is some fundamental intelligence or consciousness behind existence. Science has explained extraordinary amounts about the universe, but there are still profound questions we haven't answered. I am comfortable admitting that I don't know. But if there is a creator, I sometimes wonder whether we have completely misunderstood the arrangement. Perhaps God initiated something, established the conditions for existence and simply left it to unfold. Perhaps humanity is not the centre of anything. Perhaps there is no grand human purpose, no celestial competition between good and evil, no divine requirement to spend our lives praising a creator. Perhaps the universe is simply doing what the universe does, and we are one of the many things that happened along the way. If I were to entertain the simulation analogy, perhaps God started the simulation and forgot about it. Or perhaps there was never a player sitting outside it in the first place.
## Let Humanity Be Humanity
What I find most disappointing is that we have so much to explore, understand and experience, yet we keep finding reasons to separate ourselves. We have a universe to study, oceans to explore, diseases to cure, technologies to develop and an extraordinary amount of human suffering that we could actually do something about. Instead, we spend generations fighting over interpretations of something none of us can conclusively demonstrate. Believe in God if you wish. Pray, visit temples, churches or mosques, celebrate your festivals and find whatever comfort you need in your beliefs. I have no interest in taking that away from anyone. But don't use your beliefs to decide whether somebody else deserves dignity, freedom or equality. Don't turn your God into a political weapon, and don't allow politicians to convince you that your neighbour is your enemy simply because they pray differently or don't pray at all. I don't know whether God exists, and I don't pretend to have an answer. But I do know that humanity doesn't need to agree on the existence of a creator to recognise the value of another human being. If there is a God, I hope that matters considerably more than how many times we sing praises.
