Yesterday, I witnessed a rather trivial disagreement escalate into a physical fight. Something that could probably have ended with a simple apology turned into a fistfight, and it made me think about how fragile our everyday interactions have become. Honestly, I think the smartest thing you can sometimes do is apologise, walk away and get on with your life. You never really know what another person is going through, what they are capable of doing or how far a moment of anger might take them. But I don't think the fight was entirely about what happened yesterday. I think we have gradually created a world where almost everyone is struggling, everyone is exhausted and nobody seems to have enough room to simply live. Your job isn't secure, you have bills to pay, debts to clear, responsibilities to fulfil and an endless list of things you are expected to achieve. Perhaps that trivial disagreement was simply where all that accumulated frustration finally found an outlet.

## What Exactly Are We Living For?

I know people who follow an almost military routine from Monday to Friday, exhausting themselves just to enjoy two days at the weekend. Others spend the entire weekend alone, not because they particularly enjoy solitude, but because they have absolutely nothing left to give. You work to pay bills, rest so that you can work again, and spend whatever little time remains trying to convince yourself that you are enjoying life. Your spouse is struggling too, and your children are already carrying enormous schoolbags, attending classes and studying twice as much as many of us did in primary school. What exactly are we chasing? A bigger house? A better car? Financial security that always seems another few years away? At what point do we actually get to live? Is this the dystopia we wanted? Is this what generations of human beings struggled, worked and sacrificed for? We have made extraordinary technological progress, yet somehow the ordinary business of being alive feels increasingly exhausting.

## The Fight Beneath the Fight

Perhaps that is what stayed with me after watching yesterday's incident. The fight was violent, but beneath that fight was possibly a person carrying years of frustration, insecurity, exhaustion and disappointment. I am not justifying violence; nobody should have to become someone else's punching bag because they are having a difficult life. But I do wonder how many people around us are one terrible day away from losing their temper. We are all running somewhere, competing with one another, trying to survive an economic system that constantly demands more, and often forgetting that the person standing opposite us might be struggling just as much. We have become so occupied with surviving that we barely remember how to live alongside one another. Perhaps we haven't failed because we lack technology, intelligence or resources. Perhaps we have failed because we have built a world where so many people have everything to worry about and so little to look forward to. And if a trivial disagreement can become a physical fight because everyone is already carrying too much, I cannot help asking: did we actually fail as humanity?
