A preventive, standard peace bond under the BNSS should not bar a person from future peaceful protests. Articles 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(b) of the Constitution of India guarantee the right to free speech and the right to assemble peacefully without arms, explicitly protect the right to protest. Since this bond is executed under Section 126 of the BNSS, is a statutory bond somehow superior to a fundamental constitutional right and reasonable restrictions require a nexus to public order, sovereignty, or the integrity of India; the state cannot simply designate every piece of land near Parliament as a restricted zone. Supreme Court held in Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan v. Union of India, the state cannot impose a blanket ban on protests at traditional sites like Jantar Mantar. When the state arbitrarily tries to shrink democratic space by pushing dissent miles away; defying that unconstitutional boundary is a legitimate defense of our rights.
If Gandhi, Nehru, Bose, and Patel had strictly operated within the framework dictated by the British, we wouldn't have a republic to begin with. I am not comparing these protesters to freedom fighters, but if your idea of protecting constitutional rights is bending your back to justify arbitrary state restrictions born out of cowardice, you need to critically examine your conscience and reevaluate what justice and constitutional principles actually mean.
