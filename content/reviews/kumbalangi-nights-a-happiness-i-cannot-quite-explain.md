Yesterday, I came across a post announcing that Madhu C. Narayanan's next directorial venture had completed shooting, and it made me want to write about his first film all over again. This man has directed just one feature film so far, and that film was Kumbalangi Nights (2019). I admire Malayalam cinema deeply. I genuinely believe it is one of the richest film industries in India, and I wouldn't hesitate for even a moment to call Kumbalangi Nights one of the finest films of modern Malayalam cinema. It took a collection of relatively young, emerging actors and brought them together to create something extraordinary. But what makes this film particularly special to me is the way it ends. There is a certain happiness you experience when a film concludes, something that is incredibly difficult to describe. I have felt it while watching older Malayalam classics, Dead Poets Society, Scent of a Woman and a handful of other films. It isn't simply happiness because the story has a happy ending. It is something deeper, almost as though your heart has been filled with the beauty of what you have just witnessed. Kumbalangi Nights was the last film that left me with that feeling.
## The Beauty of Ordinary People
Everything about this film felt genuine. Its characters weren't extraordinary people performing extraordinary things. They were flawed, complicated, occasionally frustrating and wonderfully human. The film didn't need to manufacture emotions or force dramatic moments to make you care about them. It simply allowed you to spend time with these people, understand their relationships and slowly become invested in their lives. The cinematography captured the beauty of Kumbalangi without making it feel like a postcard, while the writing gave every character room to breathe. There was something incredibly beautiful about watching these people find their place in the world and, more importantly, find one another. The ending was particularly satisfying because it didn't feel like a reward handed to the audience. It felt earned. And perhaps that's what made me leave the film with such a ridiculous smile.
## A Tapestry of Performances
The performances were something else. Soubin Shahir and Fahadh Faasil were undoubtedly the highlights for me. Soubin delivered one of the most heartfelt performances I have seen, while Fahadh was simply terrifyingly good. His portrayal of Shammi remains one of his finest performances, and the way he occupies the screen is remarkable. But it would be unfair to single out just the two of them. Shane Nigam, Sreenath Bhasi, Mathew Thomas, Anna Ben and Grace Antony, every single one of them brought something meaningful to the film. It almost felt as though everyone was competing with one another to deliver a better performance, except nobody was trying to outshine anyone. Their performances complemented each other beautifully. The music deserves its own appreciation too. The soundtrack and background score were so soothing, fitting into the film's atmosphere without demanding attention. Everything seemed to belong exactly where it was.
## Waiting for That Feeling Again
I honestly don't know what exactly makes Kumbalangi Nights so special to me. Perhaps it was the simplicity, perhaps the characters, perhaps the way the film celebrated ordinary people without making a spectacle of their lives. Maybe it was the satisfaction of watching people who had spent so much of their lives feeling inadequate finally recognise their own worth and the value of the people around them. There is something particularly heartwarming about seeing people find happiness without having to become someone else first. I don't know. Sometimes trying to explain why a film makes you happy is more difficult than explaining why it makes you sad. I am waiting to experience something like this again, perhaps through Madhu C. Narayanan's next film, or perhaps through something entirely unexpected. I hope his next venture is every bit as beautiful. But even if it isn't, he has already given me a film I will return to whenever I want to remember why I fell in love with cinema in the first place.
