This is probably the right time to talk about Bethlehem Kudumba Unit. I watched the movie soon after it came out, and I genuinely enjoyed it. More than anything, the movie gave Nivin Pauly the kind of comeback he had been waiting for. And boy, he didn’t waste the opportunity. He didn’t just make a comeback; he completely took over the market.
If you have followed Nivin’s career, you would know that there was a time when his stardom was massive. During the Premam period, he was a genuine sensation, and his popularity had reached a level where you could actually put his stardom alongside the big names of Malayalam cinema, including Mammootty and Mohanlal. As an actor, though, I would be a little more careful with comparisons. I wouldn’t put him alongside those legends purely in terms of acting ability. But when it comes to his own space, his own kind of characters and his particular genre, Nivin is extremely difficult to beat.
## About the Age Difference
Now, the biggest discussion around the movie has obviously been the age difference between the characters. An adult actor falling in love with a young girl and eventually reciprocating her feelings has made a lot of people uncomfortable. I understand that concern, but I don’t think every movie dealing with such a relationship should automatically be treated in the same way.
For me, there is a difference between showing something without acknowledging it and actually making the age difference part of the story. If a movie simply shows an older man falling for a young girl, presents it as completely normal, never talks about their age difference and turns the whole thing into a romantic fantasy, then yes, I think there is a problem there. Sarvam Maya, for example, did something along those lines, and I can understand why people would question that.
But Bethlehem Kudumba Unit is different in that regard. The age difference is very clearly there, and the movie itself knows it. It even makes fun of it. The same thing happened in Vellimoonga. The audience isn’t expected to forget the obvious difference between the characters.
I was also reminded of Kanamarayathu, the Mammootty and Shobhana movie, which had a somewhat similar situation. The age difference is explicitly brought into the conversation. There is even a moment where Mammootty’s character says that if he had married, he could have had a daughter her age. So the movie isn’t pretending that the age difference doesn’t exist.
That is why I don’t think the existence of such relationships in films should automatically become the issue. These kinds of relationships do exist in real life. Older people can fall in love with younger people. It happens. What matters is how the film presents it. If the movie acknowledges the circumstances and doesn’t pretend that the age difference isn’t there, I don’t see why the mere existence of the relationship should become controversial.
## Gireesh AD’s Formula Still Works
Apart from all this, Bethlehem Kudumba Unit is very much a Gireesh AD movie. One character falls in love with another, the relationship starts taking shape around the interval, something goes wrong towards the end, somebody finds out about the relationship or another conflict comes into the picture, and eventually everything comes together in the climax.
You would think that this formula would become repetitive after a while. Somehow, it doesn’t.
I have watched almost four of his five movies multiple times, and even when you know roughly where the story is going, they still feel fresh. That’s probably one of Gireesh AD’s biggest strengths. He takes a fairly familiar structure and fills it with characters, conversations and little moments that make the journey enjoyable.
## Nivin, Mamitha and the Rest
Nivin was just brilliant. More than anything, this felt like the kind of role he needed at this point in his career. Mamitha Baiju was also much better than I expected. I still don’t know how versatile she can be because I haven’t seen enough from her to make that judgement, but there is definitely something there. I wouldn’t be surprised if she ends up showing a lot more range in the future.
The supporting characters were also good, and the dialogues were one of the things I really enjoyed. But for me, the climax was probably the biggest highlight. The resolution was handled exceptionally well, and I particularly liked how respectful it was towards the characters and the situation instead of forcing some unnecessary dramatic ending.
## What This Comeback Means
But beyond Bethlehem Kudumba Unit itself, I think Nivin needed this movie. For quite some time, he had been trying different things and taking risks with his films. That’s not necessarily a bad thing, but those experiments hadn’t resulted in the kind of massive box-office success he once had. During Premam, Nivin was everywhere. He was a sensation. Then, over the years, things changed.
And now look at where he is again. Bethlehem Kudumba Unit went on to become the highest-grossing movie in Kerala by beating Vazha II. It then became the highest-grossing Malayalam movie by surpassing Lokah. And perhaps the craziest achievement is the footfall. It recorded the highest footfall for a movie since 2000, beating Narasimham. That is an almost ridiculous achievement considering how difficult it is to sustain that kind of theatrical audience in Kerala.
And honestly, I think there is something bigger to take from Nivin’s comeback than just the box-office numbers. Sometimes, you really are one good decision away from changing everything. You can spend years trying different things, watching things fail, wondering whether you have already had your best period and questioning what comes next. And then one movie works. One decision clicks. Suddenly, the same person everyone thought was struggling is back at the top again. Nivin Pauly’s comeback is probably one of the best examples of that.
