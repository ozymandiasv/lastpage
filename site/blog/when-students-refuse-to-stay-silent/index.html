When Students Refuse to Stay Silent

Why a student movement for safety deserves answers, and why outsiders shouldn't become the convenient explanation.

What happened at Lovely Professional University is disturbing, but what interests me most is the reaction of the students. The protests began after allegations that a female student had been sexually assaulted on campus by an outsider and that the hostel warden had been informed but failed to act. The university denied the allegations and initially described them as false and fabricated. Police nevertheless registered a rape case against an unidentified person and formed an SIT to investigate. At this point, the allegation itself has not been established, and that distinction matters. But so does the fact that students clearly felt that something was wrong enough to take to the streets. What makes the situation even more uncomfortable is what happened afterwards. LPU released a video featuring three students from the hostel room at the centre of the allegations, in which they said that no such incident had happened. But those same students have now appeared in another video claiming that they were instructed by university authorities about what to say and that the original video was edited and posted without their consent. These are still allegations and their authenticity and circumstances need to be investigated, but I cannot simply ignore them. If students were actually pressured into giving a particular version of events, that would raise a completely different set of questions about the university’s response.

The Outsiders Question

I support the students’ demand for answers, but I am equally uncomfortable with reports of outsiders becoming involved in what should have been a student protest. Police are investigating the possible presence of outsiders, and officials have said walkie-talkies and other material were recovered from people apprehended during the unrest. That needs to be investigated properly rather than assumed either way. A legitimate student protest should not become an open door for people with no connection to the university to enter the campus, provoke violence or hijack the issue. At the same time, the possibility of outsiders being involved should not become a convenient explanation for everything that happened.

I Am Still Proud of the Protest

The violence, vandalism, arson and attacks on police cannot be justified simply because the students had a grievance. But I don’t want those images to become the entire story either. What makes me proud is that students refused to quietly accept what they believed was an inadequate response to something that concerned their safety. That instinct to question authority is important. Maybe the allegations will ultimately prove false. Maybe parts of the story will turn out to be true. Maybe the investigation will reveal something none of us currently know. That is exactly why there needs to be a proper investigation instead of competing narratives on social media. But whatever the final truth is, students asking “What happened, and why weren’t we told?” should never be treated as the problem. Protest can be messy. It can even go wrong. But the answer to a protest demanding safety should never be silence.
