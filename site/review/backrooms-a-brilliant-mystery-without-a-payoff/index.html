Backrooms: A Brilliant Mystery Without a Payoff

An unsettling concept, brilliant suspense, and a conclusion that left me wondering what the journey was all about.

Type Movie
Rating 7/10
Stars ★★★★★★★☆☆☆

I went into Backrooms expecting a psychological mystery, and initially, I thought the entire experience might be happening inside someone’s subconscious. Perhaps it was a manifestation of their fears, memories or unresolved trauma. But as the story progressed, I began to realise that the Backrooms were real within the film’s universe, only to find myself confused again about what they actually were and how they came into existence. Even towards the end, the film leaves us wondering about the origins of this strange place. Is it supposed to represent the emotional baggage we carry, the things we suppress, or the consequences of our attempts to escape reality? The concept is fascinating, and I cannot deny that I was intrigued by what the creators were trying to explore. However, I struggled to understand what they ultimately wanted to convey.

The Suspense Was Brilliant

What I cannot deny is how brilliantly the film handles suspense. There were scenes where I was completely absorbed, waiting to see what would happen next, and I remained awake through most of the film despite its strange and deliberately disorienting narrative. The atmosphere works, the mystery keeps you engaged, and the performances are genuinely good. I particularly loved the table conversation where Clark opens up about his vulnerabilities and Mary opens up about hers. For a moment, the film feels grounded in something recognisably human, allowing its characters to become more than people wandering through an inexplicable nightmare. That scene gave me something emotional to hold on to, which made me even more curious about where the story was heading. The problem is that the film creates so much anticipation that you naturally expect all this tension to lead somewhere.

An Unresolved Ending Is Fine, But Where Is the Climax?

I don’t believe every film needs to explain everything, nor do I think an unresolved ending is necessarily a bad ending. Some stories are more powerful precisely because they leave certain questions unanswered. But an unresolved ending and an absent climax are two different things. A film can leave the origins of the Backrooms unexplained while still bringing its central conflict, character development or thematic concerns to a meaningful culmination. A climax doesn’t necessarily need a dramatic confrontation or a shocking revelation, but the narrative should give us something that makes the journey feel as though it has reached a defining point. With Backrooms, I kept waiting for that moment, for the suspense and all those strange events to converge into something meaningful. Instead, the ending felt hollow, almost as though the film had spent so much time building its mystery that it forgot to give the story a proper culmination. It is one thing to leave the audience thinking; it is another to leave them wondering whether there was ever a destination to begin with.

Magical Realism Meets Absurdist Drama

The film felt to me like a combination of magical realism and absurdist drama, where the strange and inexplicable are allowed to exist without necessarily being explained. There is nothing inherently wrong with that approach. In fact, I find the concept of the Backrooms fascinating precisely because it allows for so many interpretations. It could be a metaphor for our psychological baggage, an exploration of reality and perception, or simply an absurd nightmare that refuses to follow familiar rules. But whichever direction the creators intended to take, I wish the film had given us something more substantial to engage with beyond the mystery itself. Perhaps the ambiguity was deliberate, and perhaps there is a deeper meaning I failed to grasp. I’m not particularly disappointed because the experience itself was interesting, and the suspense, atmosphere and acting kept me invested. Nevertheless, I finished the film feeling that some important questions deserved answers, not necessarily about what the Backrooms were, but about what the entire journey was meant to accomplish. I enjoyed the suspense, admired the concept and loved the performances, but when the film ended, I was left wondering whether I had witnessed a deliberately unresolved story or a story that had forgotten to resolve itself.
